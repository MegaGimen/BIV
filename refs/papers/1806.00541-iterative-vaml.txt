Extension Complexity of the Correlation Polytope

Pierre Aboulker^1 1Laboratoire G-SCOP, CNRS, Université Grenoble Alpes , Samuel Fiorini^2 2,3,4
Département de Mathématique, Université libre de Bruxelles , Tony Huynh^3 , Marco Macchia^4 and 
Johanna Seif^5 5Département d’Informatique, Ecole Normale Supérieure de Lyon

Abstract.

We prove that for every nn-vertex graph GG, the extension complexity of the correlation polytope of
GG is 2O⁡(𝗍𝗐⁡(G)+log⁡n)2^{O(\tw(G)+\log n)}, where 𝗍𝗐⁡(G)\tw(G) is the treewidth of GG. Our main
result is that this bound is tight for graphs contained in minor-closed classes.

1. Introduction

All graphs in this paper are undirected and simple. Let G=(V,E)G=(V,E) be a graph. The correlation
polytope of GG, denoted 𝖢𝖮𝖱⁡(G)\COR(G), is the convex hull of the characteristic vectors of induced
subgraphs of GG. More precisely, 𝖢𝖮𝖱⁡(G)\COR(G) is the polytope in ℝV∪E\mathbb{R}^{V\cup E} which
is the convex hull of all vectors of the form (χ⁡(X),χ⁡(E⁡(X)))(\chi(X),\chi(E(X))) for X⊆VX\
subseteq V, where E⁡(X)E(X) is the set of edges with both endpoints in XX, and χ\chi denotes the
characteristic vector of a set. It is closely related to the stable set polytope of GG, denoted
𝖲𝖳𝖠𝖡⁡(G)\STAB(G), which is the convex hull of characteristic vectors of stable sets of GG. There
are other equivalent definitions of the correlation polytope, and it arises naturally in many
different contexts, including probability theory, propositional logic, and quantum mechanics [17].

More recently, the correlation polytope has also acquired greater prominence in machine learning,
where it is more commonly referred to as the marginal polytope. For example, Wainright and Jordan [
24] showed that the maximum a posteriori (MAP) inference problem for graphical models [16, 6, 8, 21
] is equivalent to maximizing a linear function over the correlation polytope. This motivates the
search for compact descriptions of the correlation polytope. The proper framework for addressing
such questions is the theory of extended formulations [11, 4, 5, 20, 27].

A polytope Q⊆ℝpQ\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{p} is an extension of a polytope P⊆ℝdP\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
if there exists an affine map π:ℝp→ℝd\pi:\mathbb{R}^{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d} with π⁡(Q)=P\pi(Q)
=P. The extension complexity of PP, denoted 𝗑𝖼⁡(P)\xc(P), is the minimum number of facets of any
extension of PP. If QQ is an extension of PP such that QQ has fewer facets than PP, then it can be
advantageous to optimize over QQ instead of PP.

Fiorini, Massar, Pokutta, Tiwary and de Wolf [9] were the first to show that many polytopes
associated to classic 𝖭𝖯\mathsf{NP}-hard problems, (including the correlation polytope of the
complete graph) have exponential extension complexity. Their results do not rely on any complexity
assumptions such as 𝖯≠𝖭𝖯\mathsf{P}\neq\mathsf{NP} or 𝖭𝖯⊈𝖯/𝗉𝗈𝗅𝗒\mathsf{NP}\not\subseteq\mathsf{P}/\
mathsf{poly}. In this paper, we determine the extension complexity of the correlation polytope
almost exactly.

In order to state our main results, we need some graph theoretic definitions. A graph HH is a minor
of a graph GG, denoted H≼GH\preccurlyeq G, if HH can be obtained from a subgraph of GG by
contracting edges. A class 𝒞\mathcal{C} of graphs is minor-closed if G∈𝒞G\in\mathcal{C} and H≼GH\
preccurlyeq G implies H∈𝒞H\in\mathcal{C}. In Observation 5, we note that if H≼GH\preccurlyeq G,
then 𝗑𝖼⁡(H)⩽𝗑𝖼⁡(G)\xc(H)\leqslant\xc(G). By the graph minor theorem of Robertson and Seymour [18],
the property 𝗑𝖼⁡(𝖢𝖮𝖱⁡(G))⩽k\xc(\COR(G))\leqslant k is characterized by a finite set of forbidden
minors. For more on the connection between the correlation polytope and graph minors, see [25, 26].

A tree-decomposition of a graph GG is a pair (T,ℬ)(T,\mathcal{B}) where TT is a tree and ℬ≔{Bt|t∈V⁡
(T)}\mathcal{B}\coloneqq\bigl\{\,B_{t}\bigm|t\in V(T)\,\bigr\} is a collection of subsets of
vertices of GG satisfying:

  • ∙\bullet
   
    V⁡(G)=⋃t∈V⁡(T)BtV(G)=\bigcup_{t\in V(T)}B_{t},

  • ∙\bullet
   
    for each u​v∈E⁡(G)uv\in E(G), there exists t∈V⁡(T)t\in V(T) such that u,v∈Btu,v\in B_{t}, and

  • ∙\bullet
   
    for each v∈V⁡(G)v\in V(G), the set of all w∈V⁡(T)w\in V(T) such that v∈Bwv\in B_{w} induces a
    subtree of TT.

The width of the decomposition (T,ℬ)(T,\mathcal{B}) is max⁡{|Bt|−1|t∈V⁡(T)}\max\bigl\{\,|B_{t}|-1\
bigm|t\in V(T)\,\bigr\}. The treewidth of GG, denoted 𝗍𝗐⁡(G)\tw(G), is the minimum width taken over
all tree-decompositions of GG.

Wainright and Jordan [23] proved that for all graphs GG, the (𝗍𝗐⁡(G)+1)(\tw(G)+1)-th level of the
Sherali-Adams hierarchy [22] of the 𝖫𝖯\LP relaxation of 𝖢𝖮𝖱⁡(G)\COR(G) is equal to 𝖢𝖮𝖱⁡(G)\COR(G).
It follows that 𝖢𝖮𝖱⁡(G)\COR(G) has an extended formulation of size nO⁡(𝗍𝗐⁡(G))n^{O(\tw(G))}. We now
derive an improved upper bound using results of Kolman and Koutecký [14] (see also Bienstock and
Munoz [1]).

Theorem 1.

For every nn-vertex graph GG, the extension complexity of 𝖢𝖮𝖱⁡(G)\COR(G) is 2O⁡(𝗍𝗐⁡(G)+log⁡n)2^{O(\
tw(G)+\log n)}.

Proof.

Let GG be a graph with nn vertices and mm edges. Note that 𝖢𝖮𝖱⁡(G)\COR(G) is the convex hull of all
0/10/1-vectors x∈ℝV∪Ex\in\mathbb{R}^{V\cup E} satisfying xu​v=xu​xvx_{uv}=x_{u}x_{v} for all u​v∈E⁡
(G)uv\in E(G). We define the constraint graph of 𝖢𝖮𝖱⁡(G)\COR(G) to be the graph G′G^{\prime} whose
vertices are the variables of the above system, where two variables are adjacent in G′G^{\prime} if
and only if they appear in a common constraint. By [14, Theorem 1], 𝖢𝖮𝖱⁡(G)\COR(G) has an extended
formulation of size (n+m)​2𝗍𝗐⁡(G′)(n+m)2^{\tw(G^{\prime})}. Observe that G′G^{\prime} is obtained
from GG by adding a path of length 22 between uu and vv for all u​v∈E⁡(G)uv\in E(G). It is easy to
see that if 𝗍𝗐⁡(G)=1\tw(G)=1, then 𝗍𝗐⁡(G′)=2\tw(G^{\prime})=2 and if 𝗍𝗐⁡(G)⩾2\tw(G)\geqslant 2,

Up to the constant factor in the exponent, we conjecture that our upper bound is tight.

Conjecture 2.

For every nn-vertex graph GG, the extension complexity of 𝖢𝖮𝖱⁡(G)\COR(G) is 2Ω⁡(𝗍𝗐⁡(G)+log⁡n)2^{\
Omega(\tw(G)+\log n)}.

Göös, Jain and Watson [10] proved that there exists a sequence (Gn)(G_{n}) of graphs where each
GnG_{n} is an nn-vertex graph and the extension complexity of 𝖲𝖳𝖠𝖡⁡(Gn)\STAB(G_{n}) is 2Ω⁡(n/log⁡n)
2^{\Omega(n/\log n)}. If true, our conjecture would improve their bound to 2Ω⁡(n)2^{\Omega(n)}and
it would yield explicit 0/10/1-polytopes with extension complexity exponential in their dimension,
as predicted by the counting argument of Rothvoß [19] (see [27, Problem 7]).

As evidence for the conjecture, we prove that our bound is tight for minor-closed classes of
graphs. The following is our main result.

Theorem 3.

For every proper minor-closed class 𝒞\mathcal{C}, there exists a constant c>0c>0 such that for
every nn-vertex graph G∈𝒞G\in\mathcal{C},

 𝗑𝖼⁡(𝖢𝖮𝖱⁡(G))⩾2c⁡(𝗍𝗐⁡(G)+log⁡n).\xc(\COR(G))\geqslant 2^{c(\tw(G)+\log n)}\,.

Actually, the proof of Theorem 3 shows that for every graph GG, we have 𝗑𝖼⁡(𝖢𝖮𝖱⁡(G))⩾2Ω⁡(h+log⁡n)\
xc(\COR(G))\geqslant 2^{\Omega(h+\log n)}, where hh is the maximum height of a grid that GG
contains as a minor. In virtue of the polynomial grid-minor theorem of Chekuri and Chuzhoy [2, 3],
this implies that 𝗑𝖼⁡(𝖢𝖮𝖱⁡(G))⩾2Ω⁡(𝗍𝗐⁡(G)δ+log⁡n)\xc(\COR(G))\geqslant 2^{\Omega(\tw(G)^{\delta}+\
log n)} for some universal constant δ>120\delta>\frac{1}{20}.

2. The lower bound

In this section we prove our lower bound. Let PP be a polytope. We note the following easy (and
folklore) facts. If AA is an affine subspace, then 𝗑𝖼⁡(P∩A)⩽𝗑𝖼⁡(P)\xc(P\cap A)\leqslant\xc(P), and
if π\pi is an affine map, then 𝗑𝖼⁡(π⁡(P))⩽𝗑𝖼⁡(P)\xc(\pi(P))\leqslant\xc(P). Since the extension
complexity of a polytope is at least its dimension, we also have the following easy observation.

Observation 4.

For all nn-vertex graphs GG, 𝗑𝖼⁡(𝖢𝖮𝖱⁡(G))⩾n\xc(\COR(G))\geqslant n.

We next show that the extension complexity of the correlation polytope is monotone under taking
minors.

Observation 5.

If H≼GH\preccurlyeq G, then 𝗑𝖼⁡(𝖢𝖮𝖱⁡(H))⩽𝗑𝖼⁡(𝖢𝖮𝖱⁡(G))\xc(\COR(H))\leqslant\xc(\COR(G)).

Proof.

It is easy to see that H≼GH\preccurlyeq G if and only if HH can be obtained from GG by deleting
edges, contracting edges, and removing isolated vertices. We show that none of these operations
increases the extension complexity of the correlation polytope.

projecting out xu​vx_{uv}. Moreover 𝖢𝖮𝖱⁡(G/uv)\COR(G/uv) is obtained from 𝖢𝖮𝖱⁡(G)\COR(G) by setting

{uv}\leqslant x_{v} are valid). If ww is an isolated vertex of GG, then 𝖢𝖮𝖱⁡(G−w)\COR(G-w) is
obtained from 𝖢𝖮𝖱⁡(G)\COR(G) by projecting out xwx_{w}. ∎

We are now ready to prove our lower bound for minor-closed classes.

Theorem 6.

For every proper minor-closed class 𝒞\mathcal{C}, there exists a constant c′>0c^{\prime}>0 such
that for every nn-vertex graph G∈𝒞G\in\mathcal{C},

 𝗑𝖼⁡(𝖢𝖮𝖱⁡(G))⩾2c′​𝗍𝗐⁡(G).\xc(\COR(G))\geqslant 2^{c^{\prime}\tw(G)}.

Proof.

As shown by Demaine and Hajiaghayi [7] (see also Kawarabayashi and Kobayashi [13]), since our
initial graph GG belongs to a proper minor-closed class, GG contains Gt,tG_{t,t} as a minor, where
t=Ω⁡(𝗍𝗐⁡(G))t=\Omega(\tw(G)). Moreover, observe that there exists h=Ω⁡(t)h=\Omega(t) such that if
HH is the grid with gadgets of height hh, then H≼Gt,tH\preccurlyeq G_{t,t}. Let HH be the grid with
gadgets of height hh. Since the extension complexity of the correlation polytope is minor monotone,
𝗑𝖼⁡(𝖢𝖮𝖱⁡(H))⩽𝗑𝖼⁡(𝖢𝖮𝖱⁡(G))\xc(\COR(H))\leqslant\xc(\COR(G)). Therefore, it suffices to show the
theorem for HH.

The idea is to describe a face of 𝖢𝖮𝖱⁡(H)\COR(H) which projects to the correlation polytope of the
complete bipartite graph Kh,hK_{h,h}. The projection is defined in such a way that the vertices of
the bipartition of Kh,hK_{h,h} correspond to bottom and left vertices of the grid with gadgets, and
the edges of Kh,hK_{h,h} correspond to the dotted diagonal edges of Figure 1. Roughly speaking, the
face is defined in such a way that the value of the variable for each bottom and each left vertex
of the grid propagates along the corresponding vertical and horizontal path. The gadgets make sure
that propagation along vertical paths does not interfere with propagation along horizontal paths.

The gadget used in the proof is inspired by the crossover gadget of the reduction from 3-SAT to 
Planar 3-SAT in [15]. In the crossover gadget (see Figure 2(b)) the square vertices represent the
clauses of a SAT formula and the round vertices represent the variables. When a round vertex is
adjacent to a square via a blue-dashed (resp. red-dotted) edge, this means that the corresponding
variable (resp. negation of the variable) appears in the corresponding clause. The main property of
the crossover gadget in Figure 2(b) is that if all clauses in the crossover gadget are satisfied,
then bb is true if and only if tt is true and ℓ\ell is true if and only if rr is true.

We simulate each copy of the crossover gadget with a copy of a fixed planar graph, which we call
the graph gadget. Each copy is independent from the other copies: all the new vertices and edges
that are created in the process are local to the copy. We ensure that the graph gadget behaves
exactly like the crossover gadget by restricting to an appropriate face of 𝖢𝖮𝖱⁡(H)\COR(H). For each
variable ii in the crossover gadget, we interpret ii as being true or false, according as, the
corresponding variable xix_{i} of 𝖢𝖮𝖱⁡(H)\COR(H) is equal to 11 or 00. The grid with gadgets HH in
